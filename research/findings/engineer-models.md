# Engineer models: does a non-Claude model earn a slot in an autonomous Claude Code engineer, where, and through which mechanism?

John's "Gemini Argon 4" is real. Google calls it "Gemini 4 Argon" (`gemini-4-argon`) and made it generally available on the Gemini API on 2026-10-13 (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog). It has a 1,048,576-token unified input-plus-output window (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-4-argon). It costs $2 in / $10 out per 1M tokens through 2026-11-30, then $4 / $20, and it is not on the free tier (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing, https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/). Paid Gemini API use is not used to improve Google's products (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms), and Gemini CLI "has not been optimized for Gemini 4 Argon" (https://geminicli.com/). A 1M window by itself does not earn Argon or any other model a slot. Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 already have 1M windows at standard price (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows). Reliability also falls near 1M. Opus 4.6 scores 93.0% at 256K and 76.0% at 1M on MRCR v2 on Anthropic's harness, and Gemini 3 Pro scores 24.5% at 1M on a different harness (https://klu.ai/benchmarks/mrcr-v2). Across 26 models the mean relative decay from 128K to 1M is 24.3% (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2605.28079), and successful agent runs stay under 20k-30k tokens (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16069). The evidence on second opinions is split. A preregistered replay finds that the gain credited to model diversity "is mostly the gain of an independent second look, which a fresh same-model session provides" (https://stephens.page/blog/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family/paper/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family.pdf). A vendor study and a multi-family panel favour a second family (https://www.greptile.com/blog/model-inversion, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20519584). Routing proxies are ruled out on mechanism. Anthropic "doesn't support routing Claude Code to non-Claude models through any gateway" (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway). A gateway credential replaces the subscription login (same URL). Gateway sessions lose HIPAA eligibility (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol). The lowest-upkeep path is a read-only CLI subprocess: the official `openai/codex-plugin-cc` (https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc README.md, commit db52e28) or `codex exec`, which runs in a read-only sandbox by default (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode). Verdict (synthesis of the three workers' inferences): multi-model routing does not belong in version 1 as a routing layer. Version 1 runs Claude only, with a fresh-context same-model reviewer. One optional, off-by-default cross-family reviewer (Codex through the official plugin) may be added for non-health code after a spike. Argon waits for a spike on video, PDF and very-long-output jobs on a paid key only. Health data stays Claude-only. Overall confidence: medium.

### Q3. Where do large-context models help an engineer, and how reliable are they near 1M tokens?

**Confidence: medium.** The numbers come from vendors and aggregators on different harnesses, and none covers Opus 5.5, GPT-6 or Gemini 4.

- **Degradation is general.** "LLMs do not maintain consistent performance across input lengths." and "Even a single distractor reduces performance relative to the baseline" (https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot, 18 models of 2025 vintage) [E22, E23]. The same report says "Claude models consistently exhibit the lowest hallucination rates." The worker calls this quote a fetch-model paraphrase [E24]. Earlier findings frame rot as "a conditional reliability distribution over model, task, scaffold, and context—not a single window threshold" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17937v2, via /home/user/Autonomy-Research/research/findings/engineer-context.md:221) [E84].
- **Near 1M.** On MRCR v2 8-needle, Opus 4.6 scores "93.0% at 256K; 76.0% at 1M" on Anthropic's harness, and Gemini 3 Pro "45.4% at 256K; 24.5% at 1M" on the contextarena.ai harness (https://klu.ai/benchmarks/mrcr-v2) [E25, E26]. An aggregator reports that GPT-5.4 "512K-1M falls further to 36.6%" and that Opus 4.6 is "the only model maintaining 70%+ performance" at 256K-1M (https://yage.ai/share/long-context-benchmark-en-20260315.html) [E27, E28]. ATLAS (26 models, 8K-1M) finds the ranking flips by length, "Claude-Opus-4.6 leads at 1M", with a mean decay of 24.3% from 128K to 1M (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2605.28079) [E29, E30]. Decay depends on the task: Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview's "Retrieval score falls 41.5%" while its in-context-learning score holds (same URL) [E31]. In a monitoring task, Opus 4.6's recall falls from 99.7% at 100K to 69% at 800K (/home/user/Autonomy-Research/research/findings/engineer-context.md:222) [E32].
- **Whole repo in context vs agentic search.** "successful agentic trajectories typically remain under 20k-30k tokens" (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16069) [E33]. Single-shot work at 64k fails for the small models tested, even with perfect retrieval recall (same URL) [E34]. Compressed context matches whole files at about a quarter of the tokens: "19,300 context tokens per resolve vs. 93,700". That study's limits are oracle-localised arms, Python only and a single model family (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09691) [E35, E36]. Coding agents with file-system tools match full-context GPT-5 on long documents (61.50 vs 61.00 on LongBench-v2), and retrieval tools "does not consistently improve performance and can even degrade it" (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20432) [E37, E38, E39]. The earlier findings note that "the degradation shape for current models in the 200K-1M range is unknown" (/home/user/Autonomy-Research/research/findings/engineer-context.md:236) [E40].
- **Logs.** "Context window constraints limit no-RAG approaches to 0.6–4.3% of available logs (22–158 out of 3,694)." (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18196v3). This comes from the Claude Sonnet 4 era with small windows and does not test 1M [E56].
- **Screenshots and UI.** "The best models catch roughly two fifths of known issues." and "Abstention is basically absent." (https://blog.scrymore.com/blog/introducing-scry-design-diff-eval/) [E53, E54]. On the vendor-run table of 311 mobile UI pairs, Claude Fable 5 scores 40.4% recall, Gemini 3.5 Flash 37.5% and Codex GPT-5.5 37.3% (same URL) [E55].

**Contradictions.** (1) Opus 4.6's 76% at 1M comes from Anthropic's internal harness, which includes problems beyond the public 1M limit. The Gemini and GPT numbers come from contextarena.ai or vendor reports, and Klu itself says "Don't rank across harnesses" (https://klu.ai/benchmarks/mrcr-v2). ATLAS, an independent 26-model suite, also puts Opus 4.6 first at 1M (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2605.28079), which supports the direction but not the exact gap. A same-harness rerun on current models is still open. (2) yage.ai lists Gemini 3 Pro at 24.5% in one place and 26.3% in a timeline table, so the page is inconsistent with itself (https://yage.ai/share/long-context-benchmark-en-20260315.html). This is unresolved but immaterial to the verdict. (3) arXiv 2505.08120 reportedly finds a long-context model with the whole repo beating Agentless scaffolds. That paper was seen only as search text, so only the opposing side (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16069) is cited. This is unresolved (open questions).

### Q4. Does a second-opinion model from another family help, and when?

**Confidence: medium.** Most of these studies are single-system, self-published or vendor-run.

- **Fresh same-model look.** A preregistered replay of 183 reviews (Claude Opus 5.5 vs Codex, GPT-6.1 Sol) concludes that "the gain usually attributed to model diversity is mostly the gain of an independent second look, which a fresh same-model session provides" (https://stephens.page/blog/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family/paper/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family.pdf) [E41]. A second look found real missed bugs in more than half of 30 features from either family (Codex 16, fresh Claude 19), "with largely disjoint find ings" [E42]. Finding a bug is not the same as fixing it: for escaped bugs that in-session reviews had raised, "the authoring agent declined both" [E43].
- **Planted errors.** "A lightweight cross-model reviewer scores no higher than same-model review." In the same study, "one CCR plus one XMR review covers more ground-truth errors than two CCR reviews (56.7% vs. 42.7%)" and "For code artifacts, CCR outperforms all XMR conditions" (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01471v1) [E48, E49].
- **Cross-family evidence.** In a Greptile vendor study, "both models find more bugs in code written by the other model than in code they wrote themselves." (https://www.greptile.com/blog/model-inversion) [E44]. Its page shows four figures (53.7%, 60.0%, 62.0%, 50.5%) without per-model labels in the text, so which model and which authorship each figure belongs to is not stated and the per-model split is uncertain [E45] (corrected after verification, 2026-10-16). In a multi-family panel (294 confirmed issues, 15 model versions), coverage rises "from about 47% with one reviewer to about 72% with a second (the largest single step)". The panel adds that "different providers are a sensible default this corpus cannot prove beats re-runs" (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20519584) [E50, E51].
- **Asymmetry.** On LiveCodeBench, "Claude Opus 4.7 review raises Codex GPT-5.5 drafts from 71.6% to 89.7%" while "Codex GPT-5.5 reviewing Claude Opus 4.7 drafts drops the pass rate from 91.4% to 82.8%". The authors note that the design "cannot fully separate the effect of review direction from the effect of that baseline gap" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21656v1) [E46, E47].
- **Architect/editor pairs.** "o1 paired with Sonnet didn't produce better results than just using o1 alone." (https://aider.chat/2025/01/24/r1-sonnet.html, 2025-01) [E52].
- **Earlier findings.** For unattended runs, Anthropic recommends that "a verification subagent or a dynamic workflow that checks its own findings has a fresh model try to refute the result" (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices, via /home/user/Autonomy-Research/research/findings/engineer-context.md:226) [E85].

**Contradictions.** (1) Greptile (https://www.greptile.com/blog/model-inversion) and the Zenodo panel (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20519584) favour a second family. The Stephens replay (https://stephens.page/blog/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family/paper/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family.pdf) and arXiv 2610.01471 (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01471v1) find fresh same-model review equal or better on code. For code, the evidence leans to the fresh same-model reviewer, for three reasons: one of those two sources is preregistered, the other uses a planted-error ground truth, and Greptile is a vendor of review products whose ground truth it built itself. Even so, 2610.01471 also shows that one same-model plus one cross-model review covers more than two same-model reviews. The question stays open until a test runs on John's own PRs with a fresh same-model control (spike S3). (2) JHU (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21656v1) finds Codex reviewing Claude harmful, while Greptile finds both directions help. Per the JHU authors, the gap in baseline strength explains the asymmetry. JHU's reviewer did not execute tests, and Greptile's reviewers are full agents.

Worker inferences (long-context note):
- Default to a fresh-context Claude reviewer subagent with test output attached, placed after the whole feature, under a rule that every finding becomes a test or a recorded decline.
- Add a cross-family reviewer only if its cost is trivial, and only at top tier.
- Cross-family review is more promising for documents and plans, and when the author model is the weaker of the pair.

### Q5. Which integration mechanisms exist, what upkeep do they need, and how do they fail?

**Confidence: medium-high** on the gateway constraints. **Medium** on CLI wrappers, since no measured results were found.

- **Routing proxies and gateways.** Anthropic "doesn't support routing Claude Code to non-Claude models through any gateway", and the gateway operator must keep up with each Claude Code release (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway) [E57]. A gateway credential or apiKeyHelper replaces the Max login: "the subscription's usage limits don't apply to them". Setting only ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL keeps the login (same URL) [E58]. Gateways that strip headers or rewrite bodies cause "hard `400` errors" and can bill cached prompts as uncached (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol) [E59]. OpenRouter's own guide says Claude Code "may not work correctly with other providers." (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/guides/claude-code-integration) [E61]. claude-code-router (MIT, last commit 2026-10-10) offers "retries, credential pools, key rotation, and ordered fallback models" (https://github.com/musistudio/claude-code-router README.md:36, commit 057abaa) [E64, E74].
- **Terms on credentials.** Anthropic does not permit routing "through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users", and Agent SDK builders should use API keys (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance) [E62]. Using Gemini CLI OAuth through third-party tools "is a violation of applicable terms and policies." (https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli docs/resources/tos-privacy.md:9, commit b25669d) [E71].
- **CLI subprocess.**
  - `codex exec` is the headless mode. It runs "in a read-only sandbox" by default and takes `--json`, `--output-schema` and `-o`. `CODEX_API_KEY` is set for one invocation only (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode) [E67].
  - It "prints only the final agent message to stdout" and streams progress to stderr (same URL) [E73].
  - The official Codex plugin for Claude Code offers `/codex:review`, `/codex:adversarial-review` and `/codex:rescue`. It works with a "ChatGPT subscription (incl. Free) or OpenAI API key." It is Apache-2.0, last commit 2026-07-07 (https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc README.md, commit db52e28) [E68].
  - Gemini CLI headless mode is triggered by `-p` or a non-TTY (https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli docs/cli/headless.md:6, commit b25669d) [E72]. It works only on a paid key after the 2026-06-18 cutover [E69].
- **Multi-model MCP.** PAL MCP (formerly Zen) is Apache-2.0 and supports Gemini, OpenAI, Azure, X.AI, OpenRouter, DIAL and Ollama. It reads keys from `.env` or the MCP `env` block, and its `clink` tool "connects external AI CLIs directly into your workflow". Its last commit was 2025-12-15 (https://github.com/BeehiveInnovations/pal-mcp-server README.md:26, commit 7afc7c1) [E65]. Each extra tool "consume[s] valuable context window space even when not in use" (README.md:207) [E66].
- **Agent SDK in-process tool.** `createSdkMcpServer` with `tool()`, where "The server runs in-process inside your application, not as a separate process". A handler can return `isError: true` so Claude can retry or explain (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/custom-tools) [E63].

**Contradictions.** None on mechanics. There is a tension: claude-code-router (https://github.com/musistudio/claude-code-router README.md:36) and OpenRouter advertise non-Claude models behind Claude Code, while Anthropic says it does not support that (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway). OpenRouter's own page concedes it "may not work correctly with other providers" (https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/guides/claude-code-integration). The evidence favours Anthropic's position for the engine.

Worker inferences (integration note). Ranking: (1) a CLI subprocess wrapped as a plugin or skill; (2) an own Agent SDK tool or small MCP that holds the data-class gate; (3) PAL MCP only as a reference or fallback (stale since December 2025); (4) claude-code-router only for a separate non-Max experiment; (5) LiteLLM rejected. Upkeep is lowest for the official plugin. Each extra vendor adds a key, a terms set and a failure mode. CLIs hold their own login on disk, and API keys belong only in the wrapper's process environment.

Worker inferences (integration note), the marking scheme:
1. Every data directory or file carries a class: `public`, `internal`, `customer` or `health`. A `model-routing.json` allow-list maps each class to its permitted endpoints (for example `health: [anthropic]`).

The candidate and integration positions remain the right shape for an optional add-on once a spike justifies it.

## Implications for the autonomous Claude Code engineer

Requirements in EARS form. Each requirement rests on the evidence cited, and the design choices are worker inferences as marked in Q5-Q8.

1. **One engine, no gateway.** The engineer shall run Claude Code on John's Max login with no LLM gateway or router in front of it. Reason: gateways to non-Claude models are unsupported, they replace the subscription login, and they leave the HIPAA configuration (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/llm-gateway-protocol).
2. **Window discipline.** When a task needs more context than about 128K-256K tokens, the engineer shall use Explore subagents and targeted reads instead of loading the whole repo or raw logs. Reason: decay to 1M and short successful trajectories (https://klu.ai/benchmarks/mrcr-v2, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2605.28079, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16069, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18196v3). The threshold is a worker inference to be confirmed by spike S1.
3. **Review.** When a feature is complete, the engineer shall run a fresh-context Claude reviewer subagent with no write tools and with test output attached. Every finding shall become a test or a recorded decline (https://stephens.page/blog/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family/paper/fresh-eyes-beat-a-second-family.pdf, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01471v1).
4. **Optional cross-family reviewer.** Where the cross-family reviewer is enabled, the engineer shall call it only through `openai/codex-plugin-cc` or `codex exec` in its read-only default, and shall write its output to a dated file (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode, https://github.com/openai/codex-plugin-cc README.md). It shall stay disabled until spike S3 shows added findings.
5. **Data-class gate.** When any input path to a non-Claude model is unlabelled or classed `health`, the wrapper shall refuse the call and log the refusal. Reason: unpaid Gemini terms (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms); BAA scope (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance). The marking scheme is a worker inference.
6. **Free tiers.** The engineer shall never hold a free-tier Gemini key or a Google-account Gemini CLI login (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms, https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli docs/resources/tos-privacy.md:34).
7. **Fallback.** When a second-model call returns empty output, times out, or fails on auth or quota, the engineer shall proceed with Claude alone and record "second opinion unavailable: <status>" in the PR or plan file, with at most one retry (worker inference).
8. **Keys.** The engineer shall pass vendor API keys only to the single wrapper process for one invocation, never as job-wide environment variables (https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/non-interactive-mode).
9. **Re-measure.** When a new Claude, Gemini or OpenAI model is released, the engineer's owner shall re-run spikes S1 and S3 before changing any routing (worker inference, consistent with the tier-measurement loop in /home/user/Autonomy-Research/research/findings/engineer-context.md:68).
